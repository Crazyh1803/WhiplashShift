- **Availability and recommended approach:** I'm available to lead the asset work. Don't model the people from primitives in `bpy`. That is how the last pass ended up with mannequins. Start from a CC0 base human that already has proper topology and UVs: Blender's Human Base Meshes pack, or MPFB2 output. Pose it with a rig and apply the armature before export. Build the clothing as a separate shell with solidify and sculpted major folds. Then bake high-to-low normal and AO maps. Record the base mesh's source and license in the manifest. **Tradeoff:** we depend on an external base asset instead of a fully procedural generator. **Alternative:** a procedural subdivision sculpt is fully reproducible, but it's very likely to look like a mannequin again.

- **3-GLB interface:** sound, with three changes:
  - Export textures as separate files (glTF "Separate" mode), not embedded. That lets Godot `.import` settings control VRAM compression and normal-map detection per file. It also lets the validator check that each file exists and its size. Embedded images hide duplicates and compression mode.
  - Export tangents.
  - Add per-material slot maps to the manifest (`albedo`, `normal`, `orm` plus colorspace). Measure the AABBs again after the Godot import, not only in Blender.

- **Texture budget (the real risk):** a 2048² RGBA8 map with mips is about 21 MiB. One uncompressed 3-map set is about 64 MiB, so two sets break the 128 MiB cap. BC7/BC1 compression brings a set down to about 11–16 MiB. Pack occlusion, roughness and metal into one ORM map using glTF's R=AO, G=rough, B=metal layout. Suggested split:
  - each person: a 1k head/hands set plus a 2k clothing/body set
  - shop: two 2k trim/atlas sets, one 2k unique sign/menu sheet, and tiling 1k steel and concrete
  - expected total: about 60–85 MiB compressed

  The validator should fail if any candidate texture imports uncompressed.

- **Triangles and surfaces:** the 120k cap is comfortable. Aim for 14–18k per person (the face and hands need the density) and about 50k for the shop. Give each person 4 surfaces: skin, hair, garment, gear/footwear. Give the shop about 9.

- **Godot material losses:** glTF import doesn't carry subsurface scattering or sheen. Without SSS, skin looks waxy.
  - Codex should add a post-import override script, limited to the candidate directory, that turns on SSS for skin and adds rim/back-lighting tweaks for fabric.
  - Use roughness 0.45–0.6 and no metal on skin.
  - Make hair an opaque sculpted mass plus a normal map, with alpha-scissor fringe cards only. Alpha-blend hair breaks sorting and shadows.

- **Shadow and contact risks:** Peter-panning (shadows detaching from objects because of shadow bias) at the feet and counter will undo the grounding.
  - Bake AO into the ORM map, and add a baked contact patch on the sidewalk under the feet and support footings.
  - Night budget: at most 2 shadowed spot lights (sign and counter), at most 3 unshadowed omni lights, and adjusted per-light shadow bias.
  - Check the project's renderer. SSAO and SSS need Forward+; the Compatibility renderer silently drops both.

- **Emission and shimmer:** put emission only on the tube/lens material, with its own mask, and raise the glow threshold so the dark backing stays dark. Chopsticks, cables and fasteners will shimmer, so give them minimum widths of about 8 mm. Test with MSAA or TAA turned on in the capture.

- **Vehicle match and isolation:**
  - Codex should measure the X-17's texel density first. If the car isn't PBR-textured, the gap in realism shows the other way round, so record that.
  - Target about 512–1024 px/m on the shop and about 1500+ px/m on faces.
  - Give the candidate scene its own WorldEnvironment and keep it out of the track catalog.
  - The post-import script and the validator must not touch the shared shadowless batches or the race loading path.
