**Recommendation:** go ahead with the plan, but generate the relief by subdividing each existing flat quad. Do not re-sample the centreline. Use a world-metre height field `h(s, u)`, where `s` is distance along the run and `u` is the lateral offset in metres, and apply hard limits that a test enforces.

**Construction**
- In `_build_run` (`track_builder.gd:180`), relief runs subdivide each `al/ar/bl/br` quad bilinearly: 4 steps along (0.875 m) by 8–10 across. The side edges and the end edges of the run then land on the original flat lines, so neighbouring flat runs get no cracks or T-junction gaps.
- Multiply `h` by `smoothstep` fades: 3.5 m (one sample) at each run end, and 1.0 m in from each road edge. The value must be exactly 0 at the boundary, not just close to it.
- Ceramic, ice, oil and curbs keep running the current code path unchanged, so the golden flat collider hashes still apply.
- Pass the same `verts` to both the mesh and `concave.set_faces`, as now. Replace the constant `Vector3.UP` normals (`:199`) with analytic gradient normals. Without that the relief still looks flat, which is the complaint you're trying to fix.
- Seed from track id, run index and surface id with a local hash. Don't use global RNG state.

**Bounds, set by the single-ray wheels**
- Each wheel casts one ray, and its reach is `susp_rest + radius`: 0.64 m for A and 1.02 m for B (`wheel.gd:179`, `tuning.gd:34-49`). The damper responds to Δcompression per step. At 50 m/s the vertical rate is speed × slope, so **slope matters more than height**.
- Suspension force acts along `contact_normal` (`vehicle.gd:810`), so any lateral slope becomes a sideways push.

| Surface | Peak \|h\| | Min wavelength | Max slope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gravel | 0.03 m | 2.0 m | 0.06 |
| Mud (soft ruts) | 0.05 m | 3.0 m | 0.06 |
| Boulder | 0.08 m | 2.5 m | 0.08 |
| Water ripple | 0.02 m | 4.0 m | 0.04 |

- No feature shorter than about 4× B's wheel radius. One ray aliases on narrow spikes.
- Every triangle must have `normal.y ≥ 0.995`. Don't animate the water collider; any motion stays in the shader.

**Main tradeoff:** relief changes how A performs on B surfaces even with coefficients untouched. That's the point, but CPU target speeds and the race/lap regressions will move. Compare them against tolerance bands, not exact values, and report the change.

**Alternative worth considering:** keep the collider flat and put the relief only in the visual mesh, with B's suspension visuals faking the response. It carries zero physics risk, but the frame change still isn't felt, so I'd only use it as a fallback if the A regression goes badly.

**Risks**
- Mud ruts lined up with the wheel tracks can steer the car through the normal force. Keep the rut profile laterally symmetric and test drift with no steering input.
- The chassis collider must clear the 0.08 m crests when A's suspension is fully compressed. Check this rather than assume it.
- `track_surface.gdshader` may assume a flat world-y. Check it for triplanar projection or water y-tests.
- Item drop and placement rays (`race_items.gd:435`) now land on uneven ground. Make sure items sit on the surface rather than floating or sinking.
- Triangle count goes up about 40–60× on relief runs. That's fine for a trimesh, but measure physics step time.

**Deposits:** use one MultiMesh per surface, kept visual-only or given simple convex colliders tagged `OFF`. Reuse the clearance check at `:166`, adding the deposit's footprint radius to `w/2 + RAIL_OFFSET + 3.2`. That check currently treats each deposit as a point, so a large heap could poke into a nearby branch.

**Regression requirements**
1. Determinism: two builds produce the same hash.
2. Over a 0.1 m grid, every `|h|` and every gradient stays within the table.
3. Run-end edges match the flat build to within 1e-5. Edge `h` is exactly 0.
4. All triangles have upward normals and nonzero area.
5. Mesh vertices equal the collider faces.
6. Downward rays every 0.25 m across each joined run all hit, and there's no gap at the seams.
7. A and B each drive straight at 50 m/s over every relief surface. No wheel loses contact for more than 2 steps, roll stays under 5°, and lateral drift stays under 0.3 m.
8. Smooth-surface collider hashes are unchanged.
9. The existing physics, race and CPU suites pass with tolerances.
10. Driver-height captures go through `ak-look`.
