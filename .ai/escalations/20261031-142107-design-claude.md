**Recommendation:** raise the caps. Set `GLOBAL_BUDGET` to 1,500,000 and `QUARTER_BUDGET` to 400,000 (`city_realism_layout.gd:4-5`). Leave `NEAR_BUDGET` (320k), `SURFACE_BUDGET` (160) and the 12 lights unchanged. This is a smaller and safer change than reauthoring the sidewalks and sliced meshes.

**Why it's enough:** the frame cost is already limited by `_fits_visible` (`city_realism_layout.gd:303-319`). That check, not the total cap, decides what draws at any one time. The 900k/240k caps only limit how much the whole map holds. Nothing in the plan ties them to a new user requirement; they are left over from the smaller pass. Four quarters at 400k gives 1.6M, so the 1.5M global cap stays the one that binds first. That keeps the density spread out instead of letting one quarter absorb it all.

**Main tradeoff: memory and load time, not draw cost.** The `_batch` function (`city_realism.gd:65-102`) doesn't instance anything. It copies every placement's geometry into a per-chunk `ArrayMesh` with `SurfaceTool.append_from`, and the `MultiMesh` holds only one instance. Reusing a family therefore saves authoring and generation compute, but no memory. Static geometry grows in step with the total cap, about 1.7× at 1.5M. On top of that:
- Far-LOD copies of every building (`city_realism.gd:46-48`) are not counted in `GLOBAL_BUDGET`.
- Godot keeps CPU-side copies of the mesh arrays.
- The GDScript `append_from` work at city build time grows in step too.

Shared materials keep the surface count per chunk flat, so the draw-call count barely moves. Denser chunks do mean coarser culling: a whole chunk draws if any part of it is visible.

**LOD and near-check risks:**
1. Near chunks cast shadows (`city_realism.gd:101`), and `NEAR_BUDGET` counts only the main pass. More near density means more shadow-pass triangles that the 320k figure doesn't capture.
2. `_fits_visible` tests only at `sample_positions`. Denser placement makes it more likely that a point between samples goes over 320k.
3. The LOD switch uses a 140 m visible range (+10 m margin), but the check uses 150 m and 130 m windows. That's consistent, but at the higher density, look for overlap spikes where near and far copies are both drawn during the fade.

**Validation conditions (all must pass):**
- The existing 320k/160/12 regressions pass unchanged, and the 29 original placements are still present.
- Re-run the visible check at every 10 m along the course, not just at the sample positions. The maximum must stay ≤320k, including during the LOD fade.
- Measure frame time in the driver capture at the densest chunk, with shadows on. It must stay within 10% of the 900k baseline.
- `RENDER_VIDEO_MEM_USED` and the time to build the city should grow by no more than about 1.8× the baseline. Record LOD0 and far-LOD triangle totals separately.
- Count rejections: `triangle_budget` rejections should drop to near zero, and `visible_budget` should become the main remaining rejection reason. That confirms the near check is the real limit.

**Alternative worth considering:** if the memory or load-time condition fails, switch `_batch` to real instancing: one `MultiMesh` per building family per chunk, instead of baked copies. Memory then follows the number of unique meshes rather than the number of placements, which fits a reuse-heavy plan. The cost is more draw calls, roughly one per family and material per chunk, so `SURFACE_BUDGET` accounting would need to change. Do that only if the measurements require it. Reauthoring the sidewalks and sliced meshes isn't justified by any constraint that has been measured.
