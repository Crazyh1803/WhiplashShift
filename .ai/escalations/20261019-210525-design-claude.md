I recommend the plan, with five changes listed below. The renderer is Forward+ (`project.godot` `config/features` 4.7 "Forward Plus").

**Recommended approach**

1. **One environment builder.** Add a `TrackEnvironment` helper. `proving_ground.gd:51-77` should call it instead of building its own sun and sky. Settings:
   - Background `BG_COLOR` set to black.
   - `ambient_light_source = AMBIENT_SOURCE_DISABLED` and `reflected_light_source = REFLECTION_SOURCE_DISABLED`.
   - `fog_mode = FOG_MODE_DEPTH` with black `fog_light_color`.
   - Glow on, auto exposure off.
   - SSAO and SSIL off. They only change ambient light, so with no ambient they cost time and show nothing.

   Leave the garage environment (`garage.gd:388-400`) and `blackjack_studio_finish.gd` alone. Limit the "no DirectionalLight3D" test to the race scene.
2. **Make beacons emissive, and use real lights sparingly.** Draw every cyan/amber guide light as one MultiMesh with an unshaded emissive material; that is free, and glow does the rest. Add a real `OmniLight3D` only about every 40–60 m. Spread them evenly by distance along the course so the results are deterministic.
3. **Put gameplay surfaces in emission, not just colour.** With no ambient light, albedo goes black between lights, so boost, dirt and ice become unreadable. In `track_surface.gdshader`, give each special `surface_id` branch a low emission term and add emissive edge lines on the road. Keep the existing uniforms (`surface_id`, `base_color`, `road_markings`) and the `TrackMaterials` cache key exactly as they are.
4. **Stepped lighting through a custom `light()` function.** Once you define `light()`, Godot's built-in diffuse and specular are dropped. You must quantise `ATTENUATION` yourself and write `SPECULAR_LIGHT` yourself, or the bone ceramic will look flat.
5. **Coral as a `StandardMaterial3D`, not a custom shader.** Use two cached `NoiseTexture2D`s with different seeds: one with `as_normal_map = true`, and one for roughness with `roughness_texture_channel` set. Turn on `subsurf_scatter_enabled` and clearcoat for the wet look. Keep it opaque. Opaque ribs also give stable silhouettes and avoid sorting problems.

**Light budget (Forward+)**

| | Limit |
|---|---|
| Clustered elements | 512 per view by default (`rendering/limits/cluster_builder/max_clustered_elements`). The per-mesh limit of 8 lights applies only to the Mobile renderer. |
| Lights visible at once | About 32–48. Set `distance_fade_enabled` to begin around 80 m. |
| Lights overlapping any one pixel | 4–6 at most. |
| Shadowed omni lights | 0–2. Each one re-renders its shadow cubemap whenever a car moves inside its range. |
| Lights per car | One small unshadowed omni (8 for a full field), or cars go fully black between beacons. |

**Renderer limitations to flag**
- Subsurface scattering is screen-space, works only on opaque materials, and shows only where a light reaches. Its transmittance (backlit glow) uses shadow maps, so on unshadowed lights it will look weak. Treat it as a bonus; don't build acceptance criteria on it.
- `NoiseTexture2D` generates asynchronously. Tests must wait for `changed` before checking texture data.
- Headless tests use a dummy renderer. Check lights, environment and materials by inspecting the scene graph. Lighting quality can only be judged from rendered captures plus `ak-look`.

**Preserving collisions and surfaces**
- Ribs and sinew must be visual meshes only. Keep using `clear_of_course` (`track_scenery.gd:103`).
- Add no new physics bodies near the road. Players would see solid-looking ribs they can drive through, or worse, hit new colliders.
- Compare the collision output before and after against `tests/fixtures/track_collision.json`, including the `surface_type` metadata.

**Main tradeoff:** real lights give dynamic light on the cars but cost GPU time per pixel and keep the budget tight. Emissive beacons are free but light nothing around them.

**Alternative worth considering:** compute the beacon light falloff in the road shader from a uniform array or data texture of beacon positions, and use real lights only on the cars. That costs almost nothing and looks the same at any distance, but the scenery and coral would get no beacon light.

**Risks to existing systems**
- Car materials tuned for sky reflections will look dead.
- The HUD's surface readout is unaffected.
- The CPU drivers' racing-line logic is unaffected.
