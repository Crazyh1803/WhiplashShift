**Recommendation: treat each oil patch as a persistent, place-anchored entity. Contact should depend only on the patch's own position and route coordinate, never on the owner's state.**

**Contact rule** (in `_tick_hazards` at `race_items.gd:404`, oil branch only):
1. **Store the anchor at drop time.** In `_drop` (`:399`), add `"route": race.entrants[index]["progress"].route_distance` to the hazard. Oil gets no `"life"` key.
2. **Gate by route position, not owner.** For oil, replace `_same_section(i, owner)` at `:413` with a wrapped gap check: `|victim.route_distance - hazard.route| ≤ 40 m`. The current check follows the owner: once the owner is more than 160 m away, its oil is dead for everyone, and while the owner stays near, oil on other legs of the track is live.
3. **Fix height isolation.** It's broken today: `pos.y = end.y` at `:421` flattens height away completely, so oil on a bridge deck hits a car on the road underneath. Instead:
   - Find the closest point on the swept segment in XZ.
   - Interpolate the segment's y at that point.
   - Require `horizontal ≤ radius` and `0 ≤ seg_y − pos.y ≤ 2.5`. The car origin sits above the deck, and nothing below the patch counts.
4. **No teleport hits.** If `start.distance_to(end) > 12`, `continue` (skip contact) instead of collapsing to `start = end` at `:417–418`. `reset_racer` already sets `previous_positions` to the current position, so the first tick after a recovery is a point test at the spawn. Also skip that tick (`protection[i] == RECOVERY_PROTECTION`) so a racer that respawns on oil isn't hit.
5. **Consume on contact.** On the first eligible contact, set `oil_time[i] = 1.2` only if `active[i] != "shield"`. Then, in both cases, set `hazard["consumed"] = true`, `_remove`, and `break` out of the cars loop. A shield therefore eats the patch without losing grip. Overlapping trail patches touched in the same tick are all consumed, which is correct.
6. **Owner exemption.** Skip `i == owner` only while `active[owner] == "oil"`. Otherwise an end swap or reversing car consumes its own fresh tail. Later on, the owner can hit its own oil like anyone else.

**Lifecycle:**
- **No expiry.** In `:408`, apply the life decrement and `available(owner)` removal to smoke only.
- **Keep oil when the owner leaves.** In `reset_racer` at `:144–146`, remove only non-oil hazards. Oil then survives the owner finishing, recovering or being reset.
- **Race reset.** `_clear_transients` (`:615`) still clears everything when the race leaves RACING. Leave it unchanged.
- **Re-deposit after consumption.** The tail check at `:283` becomes `not tail.is_empty() and not tail.get("consumed", false) and (id == "oil" or tail["life"] > 0) and dist < spacing`. Oil only skips the life refresh. A consumed tail then fails the check, and a stationary dispenser drops a new patch.
- **Caps per type.** Count by id and evict the oldest hazard *of the same id*, so smoke never evicts oil. Oil needs its own, larger cap (e.g. 512). One 5 s use at high speed can produce about 300 patches, more than the current `MAX_HAZARDS` of 192. Evicting the oldest oil at that cap is the one unavoidable exception to "persists until driven over". Check cost: 512 patches × 8 cars of segment tests per tick is cheap.

**Tradeoff and alternative:** the route gate costs one float per patch and depends on `route_distance` being stable near crossings. The alternative is a vertical gate only. It's simpler, but an at-grade crossing of another track leg could consume oil. Keep the route gate.

**Risk:** existing tests that assume oil expires after 7 s, or is removed with its owner, will fail and need rewriting, not loosening.
