I couldn't run the benchmark, so I haven't confirmed a root cause. These are ranked hypotheses from reading `track_relief.gd:15-27` and the threshold at `cpu_tests.gd:137`.

**Size of the miss.** The threshold is `(15215.05/2)*1.2 = 9129.03`. The old build passed it by only 3.6 m (0.04%). The new build misses it by 24 m (0.27%), with zero recoveries and identical first laps. That is a small, uniform pace loss, not a handling failure, and the margin was already thin enough that any perturbation could flip it.

**Hypotheses, most likely first**
1. **Ordinary vertical roughness, not lateral steering.**
   - Lateral term: the largest slope is 0.08·0.25·0.7 ≈ 0.014 (about 0.8°). At ±4 m the factor is `sin(2.8)` ≈ 0.33, so the slope is ≈ 0.005. The lateral force is `g·slope` ≈ 0.05–0.14 m/s², which is weak.
   - Longitudinal term: its slope is as large as the lateral one at full amplitude, and it acts at every lane offset. Bumps at 0.45–1.2 rad/m, driven at 13–17 m/s, give 6–20 rad/s suspension excitation. That costs a little traction and speed in the mud, boulder, gravel and water sections, where the log shows the CPUs spend most of their time.
   - Check: run the full benchmark with `0.25*cos(across*0.7)` replaced by `0.25` (longitudinal-only). If the leader stays near 9104, lateral is not the cause. If it returns to ≥9129, lateral is the cause.
2. **Chaotic sensitivity of the benchmark.**
   - Any terrain change moves swap timing and line choices. A 0.3% change over 420 s may just be noise around a threshold that sat within 0.04% of the baseline.
   - Check: run the same build with `phase` shifted (change the `seed*17` offset) over 3–5 seeds, and measure the spread of leader distance. If the spread is about ±30 m, the benchmark is measuring noise at its margin.
3. **Surface-specific effect.** Boulders have the largest amplitude (0.08). Check by scaling each surface's `MAX_HEIGHTS` entry to 0 one at a time and watching which one moves the leader distance.

**Recommendation:** make the relief longitudinal-only, but only if check 1 shows it recovers the distance.
- It removes any artificial rut-steering by construction, so the CPU's lane offset no longer matters.
- Gameplay loses nothing, because the 0.7 rad/m cross-term is a 5 cm effect.
- Implementation: use `profile := 0.5 + 0.25*cos(s*frequency+phase) + 0.25` and keep `edge_fade`. Normalize if you want the same mean (`0.5 + 0.5*cos(...)`, or whatever amplitude you prefer).
