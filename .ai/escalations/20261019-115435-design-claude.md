**Recommendation: compute the corner runs once in `CourseCues.setup()` and keep them as immutable data, then do only the speed and braking maths per frame in a static helper.**

Corner geometry doesn't depend on the vehicle, so it belongs in `setup()`, next to `events`. Grip, end and braking depend on the vehicle, so they go in `static func corner_advice(run, end, tuning, speed) -> Dictionary` and are computed each frame. That keeps the grouping deterministic and testable without a vehicle, and the per-frame cost stays at about the same O(runs) scan that `upcoming()` already does.

**Grouping corner runs**
1. **Curvature per point.** Use the signed heading change between segment `i-1→i` and `i→i+1`, computed in XZ with `atan2(cross.y, dot)`, divided by the mean length of the two segments. Per-vertex curvature is noisy, so smooth it with a distance-weighted window of about ±8 m. Weight by metres, not point count, so the result doesn't depend on sample spacing.
2. **Hysteresis.** A run starts when |κ| ≥ 0.004 and ends when |κ| < 0.003, or when the sign flips. A sign flip splits an S-bend into two runs. Gaps under about 6 m with the same sign get merged, so one bend doesn't break into fragments.
3. **Filter.** Keep a run only if its total turn angle (∫κ ds) is at least 25° and it is at least 15 m long.
4. **Seam.** Don't merge runs across the wrap after the fact. Instead, start the scan at the straightest point (minimum |smoothed κ|) and walk `posmod` indices around the lap. A bend through the finish line then comes out as one run. Store each run's `start` with `fposmod`, and check whether you're inside it using wrapped `ahead`, as `upcoming()` does.
5. **Classify from geometry only**, so labels don't change when you swap ends. Hairpin: total angle ≥ 135°. Tight: minimum radius < 35 m (tune this). Everything else is a plain left or right bend.

**Advisory speed**
- `μ = tire_grip(end) × Surfaces.GRIP[s] × mod_grip(end, s)`. Take the **minimum** over the surfaces inside the run, not the current surface.
- `v = 0.8 × sqrt(μ g / κ_peak)`. Use peak curvature, not the average, and leave downforce out so the number errs low. Clamp it to `top_speed(end) × mod_top`.
- **Braking distance:** `a = 0.7 × min(brake_force/mass, μ_approach × g)`, then `d = (v² − v_t²)/(2a) + v × 0.4 s`, where 0.4 s is reaction time. Show the slowing cue when the distance ahead is ≤ d.

**Main tradeoff:** the pure friction-circle formula ignores the steering limit (`max_steer` and `steer_falloff`) and the `slip_k` saturation. At high speed, End A can run out of steering before it runs out of grip, so the number can still be optimistic. The 0.8 factor partly covers this. The alternative worth considering is calibrating the factor per end from a scripted CPU run through each real corner. That's more accurate, but it couples the advice to CPU behaviour, so defer it.

**No misleading guarantee:** label it `≈ 72 km/h` or `BRAKE → 70`, never "SAFE". Round down to steps of 5. Hide the number entirely when μ is low (ice or oil inside the run) and show `LOW GRIP — EASE OFF` instead.

**Risks**
- `sample_at` and `upcoming` are linear scans. That's fine for now, but don't add another per-frame scan over points.
- Centreline curvature is not the racing line, so tight-but-wide corners will get conservative speeds. That's acceptable for advice.
- The HUD region is shared with the terrain cue. Decide a priority now (slowing cue > corner label > terrain) and give it a layout test.
- In tests, check that `TuningProfile` and vehicle state are unchanged by comparing property dumps before and after.

**Tests**
- A straight produces zero runs.
- A ±3° zigzag every 2 m produces zero runs.
- An S-bend produces two runs with opposite signs.
- A course whose point 0 is in the middle of a hairpin produces exactly one hairpin run.
- The same arc sampled every 1 m and every 5 m gets the same class, and peak κ agrees within 10%.
- Lower μ gives a lower speed and a longer braking distance.
- Ending A versus B changes the speed but not the class.
