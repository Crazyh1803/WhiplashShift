- **Every model must be one joined mesh, or the whole block fails to load.** `city_street_block.gd:72-75` returns false whenever `collect_static_meshes` finds anything other than exactly one mesh. People, signboards and blade signs therefore need to be joined into the single model object before export. Leaving them as separate objects or instanced collections makes the street block loader fail for the entire block, not just the missing piece. In the Blender script, assert one mesh object per model before calling `export_glb`.

- **Adding a material breaks the 8-material rule, and an unsupported material type fails the load.** Each glTF material becomes one surface, and lines 84-87 reject any surface whose material is not a `BaseMaterial3D`. Do not add per-shop materials such as `neon_red` or `neon_cyan`. Skin, clothes, hair and sign backing should reuse the existing semantic slots, with variety carried by vertex colour.

- **All neon on a model will glow the same colour.** Line 89 multiplies vertex colour into albedo only. Godot's `emission` is a single uniform colour that ignores vertex colour. Red ramen, cyan coffee and green pantry icons on the one neon material will all glow the material's single emissive colour, which loses the "distinct colour" identity the plan asks for. The smallest fix that keeps 8 materials is an emissive palette texture: a tiny swatch strip, with the neon UVs snapped onto the right swatch cell. glTF supports `emissiveTexture` and Godot imports it.

- **The low "fill" glow will wash out skin variety and dark sign backing.** Lines 91-94 give every non-emissive material a glow equal to `albedo_color × 0.12`. If `albedo_color` is white (which vertex colouring needs), every person, face and dark sign backing gets the same flat grey added on top. That reduces skin and clothing variety and the neon-to-backing contrast at night. Codex should either skip the fill or reduce it for the sign-backing and person surfaces, keyed by material name. Check this in the runtime night capture.

- **People could export with no colour at all.** Recent Blender glTF exporters only write vertex colours (`COLOR_0`) when the setting is "Active"/"Material" and the colour is actually wired into the material. If `COLOR_0` is missing, every vertex reads as white, so all people come out the same colour. Assert that every model has the attribute after export, and add a test that a person region has more than one colour.

- **Lettering is the main triangle-budget risk, not the people.** A 500-750-triangle person budget is safe (40 people is about 30k triangles), but text converted straight to mesh, or bevelled curves, can cost thousands of triangles per word.
  - Convert text with curve resolution 1-2 and no bevel.
  - Build neon tubes as 4-6-sided extrusions.
  - Triangulate and apply modifiers before counting, so Blender's counts match the decoded counts.
  - Keep each model at or under 45k triangles so there is headroom under the 50k cap.

- **Small text will be unreadable from the car or the sidewalk.** Guidelines:
  - Letters at least about 0.35 m tall, strokes at least about 5 cm wide.
  - At most 10-12 characters per line, so "BOLT & BREW" fits but avoid splitting it awkwardly.
  - Lettering offset 1-2 cm from its backing to avoid flicker (z-fighting).
  - Check for mirrored text and flipped normals, since back faces are culled.
  - There are no distance LODs, so thin tubes will shimmer at driving speed. Prefer chunkier strokes and the icon over the words.
  - Invented-glyph signs must keep the icon as the dominant shape.

- **Watch these existing assertions.** Material-name and role assertions in the current tests, and the preservation checks on the workshop and the fourteen original kit exports, will fail if the shared person or sign helpers touch those exports. Keep the helpers to the three neighbourhood models only, and re-check the `ak-blender scene_info` triangle totals against the 220k block cap.
