## Root cause (likely, not confirmed by a run)

The failure is in `preserve_paint_borders`, `build_blackjack_blueprint.py:70-71`. It protects shell vertices that lie within 0.014 of a glass surface. It does not protect the shell faces that carry the glass.

- **Large faces at the window edge.** A shell face that overlaps the window footprint can have its other vertices outside the 0.014 band. Those vertices stay collapsible, and decimation can tilt or lift the face under the glass edge.
- **Why the gap goes up and not down.** `seat_glass` (lines 93-102) seats only the glass vertices. They land on the reduced skin at `hit + normal*0.0055`.
- **Why the validator catches it.** The validator samples triangle centroids and edge midpoints (`validate_blackjack_blueprint.py:106-117`), which can fall over a tilted face that no glass vertex touched.
- **Fits the numbers.** The minimum clearance of 0.0024 shows nothing intersects. The maximum of 0.0245 is a localized lift, which is what a tilted edge face gives.
- **Fits the history.** The truck-front overshoot of 0.0107 had the same cause. The 0.0045 offset only hid it.
- **Why it is not interpolation error.** Glass is not decimated, so its triangles are small (700 samples). A gap of 0.024 cannot come from chordal error between glass vertices.

The fix is to protect the complete faces that support each window, plus a one-ring margin. Do not use a vertex-distance heuristic. Cast the glass vertices and sample points down onto the current shell, then zero-weight every vertex of each face hit. Keep the distance rule as a backstop.

## If the fix does not move the max gap

Ranked hypotheses, each with a check:

1. **The lift sits at a vertex outside the protected set.** In the validator, print the `sample` position of the max-gap triangle and the hit face's vertex indices. Compare them with the `borders` set, which you can dump from the builder.
2. **Blender moves the survivor of a collapse that has a zero-weight endpoint.** Zero-weight vertices may still be moved by a collapse with an unprotected neighbour. Check by comparing the coordinates of the protected vertices before and after `apply_modifier`. If any moved, set `mod.use_collapse_triangulate` and use `vertex_group_factor` with `invert_vertex_group` handling, or pin by snapping them back after decimation.
3. **The normal offset moves the glass sideways.** `seat_glass` uses `hit + normal*c` with the face normal, so on a tilted face the offset shifts the glass laterally. This only shows up if (1) and (2) are ruled out. Offset along the ray axis divided by `|n·d|` instead.
